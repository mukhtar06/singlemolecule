# 7-Day LinkedIn Content Plan — Portfolio Strategy, Leadership & AI

**Positioning goal:** Build external recognition as a portfolio strategy and R&D leadership voice in biotech, with AI fluency as a differentiator — laying groundwork for a future VP/C-suite move, without signaling internal ambition or flight risk to your current organization.

**Guardrails baked into every post below:**
- No mentions of internal roadmaps, pipeline specifics, budgets, or org decisions — every example is reframed as an industry-level or hypothetical/composite scenario.
- No "personal branding" language, no "on my journey to leadership," no CEO/C-suite references anywhere in the copy. The ambition shows up in the *quality and altitude* of thinking, not in stated intent.
- Company is mentioned neutrally at most once across the week (standard "where I work" context), never as the subject of the insight.
- Tone reads as "generous practitioner sharing frameworks," not "candidate marketing myself." That's what makes it land with recruiters/board members without raising flags internally.
- Posting cadence (4 original posts + 3 lower-effort engagement days) avoids the "suddenly very active on LinkedIn" pattern that invites questions.

---

## Day 1 (Monday) — Framework Post
**Purpose:** Establish authority on core portfolio strategy mechanics. This is the "save-worthy" post that gets you found by search/algorithm.

**Format:** Text post, short paragraphs, no image needed (or a simple 2x2 matrix graphic if you want to invest 10 minutes in one).

**Draft:**
> Most R&D portfolios don't fail from bad science. They fail from bad math.
>
> Three questions kill more programs (or keep bad ones alive) than any single experiment:
>
> 1. **Are we scoring probability of success honestly, or optimistically?** Teams anchor to the number that got the program funded, not the number the latest data supports.
> 2. **Is capital allocated to the portfolio, or to whoever presents best this quarter?** The loudest program in the room usually isn't the one with the best risk-adjusted return.
> 3. **Do we have a real kill criterion, or just a "let's revisit in six months"?** Vague gates are how mediocre programs survive for years.
>
> None of this requires new tools. It requires someone in the room willing to ask the boring question before the exciting one.
>
> What's the portfolio question your organization avoids asking?

**Hashtags:** #PortfolioStrategy #BiotechRnD #DrugDevelopment #StrategicPlanning
**Best time:** Tue–Thu, 8–9am local (Monday drafted, consider posting Tuesday if Monday engagement is historically weak on your profile)

## Day 2 (Tuesday) — AI/Industry Commentary
**Purpose:** Signal AI fluency applied to *decision-making*, not just lab tooling — this is the differentiator most portfolio leaders lack, and it's the exact intersection C-suite search committees now screen for.

**Draft:**
> Everyone in biotech is talking about AI for target discovery. Fewer people are talking about AI for portfolio decisions — and that's the bigger near-term unlock.
>
> The hard part of portfolio strategy was never "can we build a model." It was always: we have partial data, three internal narratives about what it means, and a board meeting in two weeks.
>
> Where I've seen AI genuinely change that conversation:
> - Surfacing comparable program outcomes fast enough to challenge an internal narrative in real time, not after the decision is made
> - Making probability-of-success estimates auditable instead of tribal knowledge
> - Cutting the time to build a defensible scenario model from days to hours, which means leadership can afford to model more scenarios, not just the one everyone already believes
>
> The models don't remove judgment from portfolio calls. They remove the excuse for not having enough information when you make one.
>
> Curious where others are seeing (or not seeing) real ROI here — versus where it's still mostly a slide in a board deck.

**Hashtags:** #AIinBiotech #PortfolioStrategy #DataDrivenDecisions #Pharma
**Best time:** Wed, 8–10am

## Day 3 (Wednesday) — Leadership / Lessons-Learned Story
**Purpose:** Humanize the authority. Recruiters and board members trust people who show judgment under real conditions, not just frameworks. Keep it composite/anonymized — never traceable to a specific current program.

**Draft:**
> Early in my career, I fought hard to keep a program alive that I believed in. I won that fight. Eighteen months later, we killed it anyway — after burning resources that could've gone to two other programs that ultimately succeeded.
>
> The lesson wasn't "I was wrong about the science." I wasn't, really. The lesson was that conviction is not a portfolio strategy.
>
> Good portfolio leadership means holding two things at once: enough conviction to advocate for programs worth fighting for, and enough detachment to recognize when your advocacy is actually just sunk-cost dressed up as scientific judgment.
>
> The best portfolio leaders I've worked with aren't the ones who are always right. They're the ones who change their recommendation fastest when the data changes — and don't take it personally.
>
> What's the hardest portfolio call you've had to reverse on?

**Hashtags:** #Leadership #PortfolioManagement #BiotechLeadership #DecisionMaking
**Best time:** Thu, 8–9am

## Day 4 (Thursday) — Curation / Engagement Day (low origination effort)
**Purpose:** This is a "visibility without output" day — critical for the algorithm and for reaching senior audiences without adding to your own posting frequency (keeps cadence from looking like a campaign).

**Actions, not a post:**
1. Find one credible industry report/article from the week (e.g., a venture report on biotech funding, an FDA policy update, a McKinsey/BCG life-sciences piece, a well-known VC's newsletter).
2. Share it with 3–4 sentences of your own POV added — not a summary, an opinion or a "here's what this means for X" angle.
3. Spend 15–20 minutes commenting thoughtfully (2–3 sentences, adds a distinct point) on posts from:
   - 2–3 biotech/pharma executives or board members
   - 1–2 people at firms you'd want visibility with eventually
   - 1 AI-in-life-sciences thought leader

**Sample share caption (if you post):**
> This tracks with what I'm seeing anecdotally: the gap between companies that treat portfolio prioritization as a quarterly ritual versus a live, continuously-updated model is widening — and it's becoming a capital efficiency story, not just a decision-quality story. Worth the read.

**Hashtags:** none needed on comments; #BiotechFunding or relevant to shared article if posting

## Day 5 (Friday) — Discussion / Question Post
**Purpose:** End-of-week engagement driver. Questions outperform statements for comments, and comments are what actually get you in front of new audiences (not just impressions).

**Draft:**
> Genuine question for people running portfolios in biotech/pharma right now:
>
> As AI tools get better at generating "the analysis," what's actually becoming *more* valuable in a portfolio leader — not less?
>
> My working theory: it's not modeling skill anymore (the tools are closing that gap fast). It's the judgment to know which model's assumptions to trust, and the credibility to say "the model says X, but I don't believe it, and here's why" in a room full of people who want a clean answer.
>
> What's your theory?

**Hashtags:** #PortfolioStrategy #FutureOfWork #AI #Biotech
**Best time:** Fri, 8–9am (Friday questions get weekend-tail engagement too)

---

## Day 6 (Saturday) — Light Engagement Only
**Purpose:** Do not post original content. Weekend posting from a "director" account reads as unusually online and invites more questions than it's worth. Instead:
- Comment on 2–3 posts from your Thursday target list that you didn't get to.
- Optionally, save/react to content for next week's Day-1 or Day-2 idea bank.

---

## Day 7 (Sunday) — Light Engagement Only / Prep
**Purpose:** Same as Saturday — no origination. Use 15 minutes to:
- Note 2–3 ideas that came up in the week's comment threads (these often make the best next-week hooks, since they're proven to generate discussion).
- Queue Monday's post so it's not written last-minute.

## Weekly Cadence Summary

| Day | Type | Effort | Visible Output |
|-----|------|--------|-----------------|
| Mon | Framework post | Medium | Original post |
| Tue | AI/industry commentary | Medium | Original post |
| Wed | Leadership story | Medium | Original post |
| Thu | Curated share + comments | Low | Share + comments |
| Fri | Discussion question | Low | Original post |
| Sat | Engagement only | Minimal | Comments only |
| Sun | Engagement only + prep | Minimal | Comments only |

**Why this cadence is safe internally:** 4 visible posts/week is active but not anomalous for a director-level professional; nothing references internal work; the strongest signals of ambition (frameworks, judgment, AI fluency) are delivered as generosity to the field, which reads as "good external representative of the company" to anyone internal who happens to see it — not as someone building an exit.

**Suggested review cadence:** After 2–3 weeks, check which post *type* (framework / AI / story / question) drove the most profile views from target titles (VP, SVP, Chief Strategy/Business/Portfolio Officer, board members, search firms) via LinkedIn analytics, and weight future weeks toward whichever is working.
